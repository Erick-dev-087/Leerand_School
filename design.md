# Leerand School Thika — Website Design Specification

## 1. Project Overview

Leerand School Thika is a CBE-based education institution positioned around whole-child development. The website should communicate that Leerand is more than academic instruction: children learn strong foundations, discover interests, innovate, participate in co-curricular experiences, and grow in confidence, character and capability.

The site is primarily parent-facing and should feel trustworthy, warm, intelligent, premium but not corporate, human, modern, confident and easy to navigate.

**Design direction:** Editorial & Storytelling, supported by modern-school clarity and subtle warmth/playfulness.

---

## 2. Brand Spine — LEARN · INNOVATE · SUCCEED

**LEARN — Building strong foundations.**  
Knowledge, understanding, skills and curiosity.

**INNOVATE — Exploring what is possible.**  
Questioning, imagining, creating, experimenting and discovering.

**SUCCEED — Growing into confident, capable individuals.**  
Success beyond grades: confidence, character, independence and capability.

These are working statements until official school wording is supplied. The layouts must allow official mission, vision, philosophy and values to replace them without redesign.

## 3. Visual System

- Royal Blue: `#123B8C` — education/content sections

- Orange: `#F58220` — warmth, creativity, action

White/cream is the major base. Navy and blue provide depth. Gold and orange are accents, not universal section colors.

### Typography

**Lora:** major/editorial headings, hero statements, pull quotes.  
**Plus Jakarta Sans:** body, navigation, UI, buttons, forms, metadata.

### Photography

Prioritize real Leerand photographs: students, classrooms, activities, community, teachers, environment. Avoid stock photography where possible.

Use photography editorially through large crops, asymmetric/organic treatments, overlaps, varied aspect ratios and subtle reveals. Avoid generic card grids everywhere.

### Motion

Motion tells the story rather than showing off:

- reveal/fade;
- subtle vertical movement;
- horizontal hero transitions;
- image mask reveals;
- scroll-focus;
- subtle image zoom;
- hover movement;
- staggered text.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

# Leerand School — Image, Layout & Responsiveness Specification

1. The overall image philosophy

Photography is not merely decoration on the Leerand website. It is one of the primary ways the site tells its story.

A parent visiting the website should see evidence of children learning, participating, creating, interacting and growing. The images should help communicate the school's character before the parent has even read every word.

Our direction is Editorial & Storytelling, with three important influences:

Editorial: large photography, intentional composition, asymmetry, generous whitespace and strong relationships between images and text.

Modern: clean alignment, clear information hierarchy and predictable interactions.

Warm and human: organic curves, varied image crops and occasional overlaps that make the site feel welcoming.

The objective is to create a coherent visual language without making every page look identical.

What we want to avoid

Every image placed inside the same rounded rectangle.

Every section built from three identical cards.

Decorative blobs surrounding every photograph.

Random image overlaps that make the content harder to understand.

Stock-looking compositions that do not reflect the actual school.

Images used merely to fill empty space.

Excessive animation that distracts from the children and their experiences.

The rule: the photograph, its content and the story being told should determine its treatment. Not every image needs a special effect.

2. Image styling system

We should use a small collection of image treatments rather than inventing a new treatment for every section.

A. Editorial photograph

Primary use: Our School, Academics, storytelling sections.

Large, carefully cropped photographs with a clear subject and enough visual space for the composition. They may use subtly rounded corners or an asymmetric crop, but should not automatically look like cards.

B. Energetic activity photograph

Primary use: Activities and co-curricular previews.

Prioritize action, movement, participation and expression. Use wider crops, closer action shots and varied image proportions. Images may bleed slightly beyond a text column or sit asymmetrically in the layout.

C. Immersive or full-width photograph

Primary use: homepage transitions, major storytelling moments.

Use when the image itself deserves to carry a section. It can stretch across a large portion of the viewport or become a visual backdrop, provided text remains readable and contrast is controlled.

D. Activity showcase image

Primary use: individual activity cards or galleries.

These images identify a specific activity quickly. The crop should make the subject recognizable even at a smaller size. A restrained hover zoom or caption reveal is appropriate.

These examples communicate the intended photography styles, not confirmed images of Leerand. The final site should use the school's real photos wherever suitable.

Organic shapes

Organic shapes are accents, not a universal image rule.

Use them selectively:

on a hero image where they reinforce the composition;

around a whole-child storytelling visual;

in one or two activity sections;

at a section boundary when it creates a useful visual transition.

Other photographs should remain clean and editorial. We want visual variety, not a page where every image has a blob, arch or wavy border.

3. Homepage: the hero image system

The homepage hero is the most important image composition on the site. It should establish Leerand's identity immediately.

Our agreed direction is:

Text on the left.

Large image composition on the right.

An editorial, slightly asymmetric layout.

A rotating selection of approximately 3–5 real school photographs.

Automatic horizontal transitions.

Manual indicators and optional arrows.

Swipe or drag support on mobile.

Working headline:

Growing Minds. Shaping Lives.

Primary CTA: Explore Leerand

Secondary CTA: Admissions / Enquire

Image selection and priority

Use this priority order when choosing the slides.

Slide 1 — Students and community

Highest priority

Start with an image that communicates the human side of Leerand: children interacting, learning together or participating in school life. This is the preferred opening image because the website is about children and their development.

Slide 2 — Learning in action

Show an authentic learning moment. The image should support the academic side of the school's identity without looking staged.

Slide 3 — Activities and discovery

Show energy, movement, creativity or participation beyond formal lessons. This signals whole-child development.

Slide 4 — Belonging and community

Use a strong group or school-community photograph if it adds a different perspective from the first slide.

Slide 5 — School environment, optional

A school/building image is secondary, not a requirement. Use it only if Leerand has a strong, representative photograph. Never force a building image into the hero just to show the premises.

The images should not all communicate the same thing. Each slide should add a different dimension of Leerand.

If we only have three strong images, use three. Quality and relevance matter more than hitting five.

Composition and cropping

The image side should feel substantial rather than like a small thumbnail beside the headline.

Implementation guidance:

Use a large image area with an intentional crop.

Keep faces and important activities within the visible crop.

Use object-fit: cover for image panels where filling the frame is important.

Adjust object-position per image; don't assume every photo should be centered.

Avoid text over faces.

Maintain enough image height to balance the headline.

Use a subtly rounded or asymmetric frame only if it improves the composition.

Do not force every slide to use the same subject placement if the source photographs differ. Use per-image focal positions.

Transition behavior

The intended behavior is a horizontal transition between slides.

Automatically advance approximately every 5–7 seconds.

Provide small indicators.

Optional previous/next controls may be used if they do not clutter the design.

Transition the image smoothly rather than flashing or replacing it abruptly.

Keep the text and image relationship stable so the whole hero doesn't feel like it is jumping.

Avoid autoplaying video or introducing complex 3D transitions.

Pause or reduce automatic motion when appropriate, and respect reduced-motion preferences.

The slide indicators should communicate which image is active. They should be visible but not compete with the primary CTA.

Mobile hero behavior

On mobile, the hero becomes a stacked composition:

Headline and supporting text.

Primary and secondary CTAs.

Large image/slideshow.

Slide indicators and any navigation controls.

The precise ordering can be adjusted if the final design looks better with the image before the CTAs, but the main message and CTA must remain easy to find.

The slideshow should:

support swipe/drag;

retain visible slide indicators;

keep the image large enough to understand;

avoid tiny, excessively wide image panels;

avoid placing text over busy parts of the photograph;

avoid taking over the entire mobile screen with a huge hero.

On narrow screens, the image can use a wider aspect ratio than the desktop portrait-like composition. Keep the focal subject visible.

4. Our School: the community photograph is intentional

This was a specific decision, not simply a suggestion.

The Our School hero should use a community photograph. Its purpose is to show that Leerand is a community built around children's growth, not merely a physical school building.

Layout

Use the same visual language as the homepage:

Text on the left.

Community photograph on the right.

Warm white/cream background.

Editorial typography.

Moderate scale rather than an oversized, cinematic hero.

Working headline:

More Than a Place to Learn.

Supporting idea:

A community built to help children learn, discover their strengths and grow into confident individuals.

The photo should ideally show several children together or a meaningful school-community interaction. A genuine photo of children engaging with teachers could also work if it feels warm and representative.

What should not be used here?

A building-only image as the default.

An unrelated stock photo.

An image that repeats the homepage hero image without a reason.

A decorative collage that competes with the message.

If the best community image has a wide composition, crop it thoughtfully rather than forcing it into a portrait frame. If a person is near the edge, preserve their visibility when the layout changes.

Responsive behavior

On mobile:

Stack the text and photograph.

Keep the headline readable without requiring several awkward line breaks.

Place the photo immediately after the introductory copy or CTA group.

Use a wider crop if that preserves the community context.

Do not hide the image simply because the layout is smaller.

The Our School hero should feel related to the homepage, but not like a copy of the homepage slideshow. A single strong community image is enough.

5. Academics: a precise alternating image/text rhythm

This page has a different job from the homepage. It explains how children learn and progress, so its design should be clear and structured.

We explicitly chose this pattern:

Image Left → Text Right

Text Left → Image Right

Image Left → Text Right

Continue alternating if more sections are needed.

This rhythm should be intentional and consistent, not selected randomly for each section.

Suggested relationship between sections

IMAGE LEFT · TEXT RIGHT

Early Years

Introduce discovery, curiosity and foundational learning. Use a photograph that shows children actively participating rather than sitting passively for a portrait.

TEXT LEFT · IMAGE RIGHT

Primary

Explain the development of strong foundations, understanding and confidence. The image can show collaborative learning, reading, practical work or another verified classroom activity.

IMAGE LEFT · TEXT RIGHT

Junior Secondary

If this stage is confirmed, show learners engaged in deeper exploration, teamwork or independent work. The photo should reflect the actual age group and school environment.

These are layout examples. The actual educational stages and the availability of each stage must be confirmed with Leerand.

Image treatment

Academics images should generally be:

calm;

purposeful;

learning-focused;

documentary rather than heavily staged;

large enough to show what the child is doing.

Use simple editorial frames, not a separate decorative card for every stage.

Avoid making every image a perfect square with identical corner radii. A shared layout does not require identical crops.

Section rhythm

Each section should have enough vertical space to feel distinct, but the page should remain compact.

The alternation provides visual movement on its own. We don't need to animate every image differently.

A subtle reveal or image-mask animation is enough. The user should not have to wait for a section to animate before reading it.

Mobile behavior

The alternating desktop layout should become a consistent stacked layout.

Recommended order:

Section heading or stage label.

Image.

Description.

Optional link or small detail.

Use the same stacking logic for every stage rather than reversing image order on alternating mobile sections. The desktop alternation provides variety; mobile should prioritize predictable reading.

Images should:

occupy most of the available content width;

use a crop that preserves the learning activity;

avoid becoming tiny beside text;

remain reasonably close to their corresponding description.

The image and its text should always feel like one unit.

6. Activities: two different kinds of photography

This distinction is important.

The Activities page should contain storytelling images and activity showcase images. They serve different purposes and should not be treated identically.

A. Storytelling images

These are the large photographs used in the main alternating sections.

Their purpose is to communicate the value of activities: confidence, teamwork, creativity, discipline, expression and discovery.

For example:

Sport & Movement

A wide action photograph of learners participating in a real sporting activity.

The accompanying text explains what participation can help children develop.

Creativity & Expression

A photograph of children making, performing, composing or expressing ideas, provided the school actually offers the activity shown.

Discovery & Thinking

A photograph of learners solving a problem, working together or exploring something practical.

These sections use the same underlying image/text layout language as Academics, but the photographs are more energetic and expressive.

B. Activity showcase images

These are smaller, more specific images that identify individual activities.

For example, if confirmed by the school:

Football

Art

Music

Athletics

Chess

Dance

Each item should quickly communicate what the activity is.

A short description can then explain the opportunity or developmental benefit.

The activity showcase is a catalogue-like component, so clarity matters more than dramatic cropping.

Suggested visual behavior

Use a clean grid on desktop if there are enough activities.

Vary image crops subtly, rather than using an identical set of rigid cards.

Keep labels easy to scan.

Use a gentle image zoom on hover.

Reveal extra text only if the interaction is obvious.

Do not make important descriptions available only on hover.

On mobile, use a two-column grid if the images and labels remain legible. If there are many activities or the available width is limited, use a single-column layout or a carefully implemented horizontal gallery.

The main story sections explain why activities matter. The showcase tells parents what Leerand actually offers. Keeping those functions separate prevents the page from becoming a repetitive list of cards.

Energetic, but not chaotic

Activities can have more visual energy than Academics through:

wider action photography;

slightly more varied image proportions;

subtle image overlaps;

different focal positions;

stronger visual accents;

occasional organic corners;

restrained scroll reveals.

Do not apply every effect to every section. One expressive image can be more effective than five layers of decoration.

Mobile behavior

Stack the large storytelling sections.

Keep the photograph and explanation together.

Prefer action images with a clear focal subject.

Ensure activity cards have readable labels without hovering.

Make horizontal galleries swipeable, with enough visual indication that more items exist.

Avoid making users horizontally scroll the entire page.

7. Why Leerand: imagery should support the scroll-focus experience

The main visual feature is the scrolling sequence:

LEARN → INNOVATE → SUCCEED

The active principle becomes sharp and readable as the previous principle recedes and the next one comes into focus.

This effect should emphasize the philosophy, not compete with it.

Relationship between text and images

We have two possible image treatments that can fit this direction:

Option 1: Typography-led focus

The principle title and its explanatory text are the main focus. A large supporting image appears beside or behind the active principle.

Option 2: Image-and-text focus

Each principle has a corresponding image. As the user scrolls, the text and image transition together from subdued to prominent.

My recommendation is Option 2 if Leerand has three genuinely different, strong photographs. It connects the philosophy to real school life. If the available images are weak or repetitive, use Option 1 rather than forcing three photographs into the experience.

Potential image relationships:

LEARN: an authentic learning moment.

INNOVATE: learners creating, exploring or working through a challenge.

SUCCEED: a meaningful group, participation or achievement moment that does not imply unverified awards or outcomes.

Scroll behavior

The active item is fully sharp and readable.

The approaching item gains prominence progressively.

The previous item fades or blurs gradually.

The transitions should feel connected rather than like three unrelated slides.

The blur must not make important text inaccessible.

Keep the sequence short enough that the page does not feel like a long animation demo.

A subtle image zoom or crop adjustment may reinforce the change of focus, but the text remains the primary signal.

Mobile behavior

The desktop scroll-focus effect may be simplified on mobile.

Priorities:

The principle is readable without precise scrolling.

The image stays connected to the correct principle.

The transition is not so sensitive that small swipes make the page feel unstable.

Reduced-motion settings disable or simplify the blur and zoom.

No content becomes inaccessible because it is outside a sticky viewport.

If a pinned/sticky sequence makes the mobile page too long or difficult to control, use three stacked editorial sections with lighter focus transitions. Preserve the storytelling idea, not the desktop mechanics at all costs.

9. Mobile and responsive rules across the whole site

Responsiveness is more than stacking columns. Image crops, focal points, section spacing, interaction behavior and reading order must all be considered.

Desktop

Use asymmetric layouts where they help storytelling.

Let important photographs occupy a substantial part of the content area.

Maintain generous whitespace.

Alternate image/text sections deliberately.

Use scroll-driven effects only where they add meaning.

Tablet

Reduce excessive whitespace before reducing image size too far.

Keep the image/text relationship intact when space allows.

Stack sections when two columns become cramped.

Adjust object-position per image instead of allowing automatic crops to cut off faces or activity.

Avoid small text columns next to large images.

Mobile

Stack text and images in a predictable order.

Keep the relationship between each image and its description obvious.

Use full-width or near-full-width images when appropriate.

Adjust image aspect ratios so they suit the available width.

Do not blindly reuse desktop image heights.

Make all controls touch-friendly.

Do not rely on hover to reveal essential content.

Keep horizontal scrolling inside intentional galleries only.

Simplify animations that depend on pinning, precise scroll positions or multiple simultaneous elements.

Cropping behavior

A photograph that looks good on desktop may crop poorly on mobile.

For each key image, decide:

what is the focal subject?

what part must never be cropped out?

can the image tolerate a portrait crop?

does the mobile crop need a different object-position?

should we use a separate mobile crop if the original image is too wide?

Do not use a single global crop setting for every photograph.

For each image, maintain useful information:

filename;

page/section;

description/alt text;

focal position;

intended desktop crop;

intended mobile crop if different;

confirmation that the image represents an actual Leerand activity/environment.

Use optimized image formats and appropriate responsive image sizes. Avoid loading every large image at full resolution on mobile.

For the homepage slideshow, load the first image promptly and lazy-load nonessential subsequent images where practical. Avoid lazy-loading an image that is immediately visible in a way that causes a visible delay.

Use meaningful alt text for informative images. Purely decorative shapes or images should have empty alt text where appropriate.

11. Final design principles to preserve during implementation

Real school life comes first. Prefer genuine Leerand photography over stock imagery.

The Our School hero uses a community photograph. The building is secondary.

The homepage hero uses 3–5 images when enough strong photos exist, prioritizing students, learning, activities and community.

Academics uses a deliberate alternating image/text rhythm on desktop.

Activities uses the same underlying layout language as Academics, but its photography is more energetic and varied.

Storytelling images and activity showcase images have different jobs. Do not style them as if they were interchangeable.

Why Leerand connects image and text focus to LEARN · INNOVATE · SUCCEED. The effect must remain readable and accessible.

Organic shapes are selective accents. Not every image needs a special silhouette.

Mobile gets its own crop and interaction decisions, not just a narrower desktop layout.

Do not force images into a layout that doesn't suit them. A strong image with a thoughtful crop is better than an elaborate treatment that hides the subject.

Use motion sparingly and intentionally. Photography and school life should remain the focus.

Never invent school activities, facilities, achievements or testimonials. Use placeholders during development and verify before launch.
